Create a Linen Handoff Note for Accommodation Supplies Delivered by a Temporary Driver

Published by Sinar Saredah Sdn Bhd.

A practical guide for Sarawak accommodation operators and venue managers on writing a linen handoff note when a temporary driver delivers supplies, separating provider confirmation, the authorised accepting person and incident reporting.

Why the handoff note needs three separate roles

A linen handoff note works best when it names three roles instead of one vague signature line. The first is the provider confirmation: the written record that the laundry service has prepared and released the linen. The second is the authorised person who accepts the items at the accommodation. The third is the person who reports an incident, such as a short count, a mismatched label or a bag that arrives in unexpected condition. When a temporary driver is involved, these roles can easily collapse into a single signature that proves nothing useful later. Keep them apart on the page. The driver is a carrier, not automatically the accepting person and not automatically the reporter. Write the note so that each role has a name field, a time field and a short statement of what that person is confirming. This structure also helps when the regular driver is away and someone unfamiliar makes the delivery. What the provider confirmation should state before the driver leaves

The provider confirmation is the part of the note that should already be filled in before the temporary driver reaches your door. Ask for it in writing through your usual approved channel, and keep it attached to the handoff note rather than relying on a verbal message. A workable confirmation states the collection or delivery reference, the date, the item categories included, and the count or set description that the provider is releasing. It should also say who at the provider prepared the record, so a later question has a named starting point. Do not ask the driver to confirm provider-side details; the driver may not have seen the preparation. If the provider confirmation is missing, mark the note as incomplete and record what you are waiting for. Your own note should distinguish between what the provider has confirmed and what your receiving staff have yet to check. This is a documentation habit, not a judgment about any particular provider. For accommodation supplies, the categories might be bed linen, towels, table linen or protectors, each with its own line. Keep the wording neutral and factual, and avoid writing assumptions about how the items were handled.

Naming the authorised person who accepts the items

The authorised accepting person is the individual at your property who is permitted to take the linen into your control. Write that role into the note before the delivery day, not after. The note should show the role title, the name of the person filling it on that occasion, and the time of acceptance. If the usual person is unavailable, the note should show who has been nominated as the stand-in and who approved that nomination. This matters most when a temporary driver arrives outside the normal rhythm of the property. The accepting person confirms only what they can actually see: the number of bags or bundles, the visible labels, and whether the delivery matches the provider confirmation. They are not confirming the condition of every item inside a sealed bundle. Keep the acceptance statement short and literal. Avoid a single signature that appears to cover provider preparation, carriage and acceptance at once. If your property uses a shared collection point, note where the items were placed and who holds the key or access responsibility, without writing access details into a document that will be shared widely.

Writing the incident report line without turning it into a blame note

An incident line should describe what was observed, when, by whom and what has been done so far. It should not assign fault to the driver, the provider or a colleague. Use plain categories: count difference, label mismatch, packaging concern, or item not on the confirmation. For each, record the reference from the provider confirmation, the accepting person's observation, and the next action with an owner. For example, a fictional note might read that two towel bundles were expected and one arrived, that the accepting person recorded the difference at the time of delivery, and that the accommodation manager will raise a written question through the approved channel. That is a complete incident line without speculation. Keep the report separate from the acceptance signature so that accepting a delivery does not look like accepting an unresolved discrepancy. Where the provider's written terms and confirmations set out how questions are handled, refer to those documents rather than inventing a process. A short worksheet you can adapt for each delivery

Use a one-page worksheet with rows rather than paragraphs. Suggested rows, offered as an author heuristic rather than a standard: delivery reference; provider confirmation attached yes or no; date and time window; temporary driver noted as carrier only; authorised accepting person and role; stand-in approval if used; item categories and counts as confirmed by the provider; counts observed at acceptance; visible label check; incident line reference; reporter and next action owner; and a closing line for when the note is complete. Keep the worksheet in a shared location that the relevant staff can reach, and decide in advance who may edit it. A second column for clarifying questions helps: write the question, the date it was sent and the channel used, then leave the answer field blank until a written reply arrives. This keeps the note honest about what is known. For properties that also send items out, the same worksheet can carry a return line, but keep outbound and inbound records visually distinct. If your property works with a laundry service across several buildings, add a building or room-block field so a bundle is never accepted into the wrong stock record.

Where this note fits alongside your other accommodation records

The linen handoff note is one record among several, and it should not try to do every job. Keep it focused on the delivery event: who confirmed, who accepted and what was reported. Link it by reference to your linen stock record, your replacement worksheet and your provider correspondence, rather than copying those details into the note. When a temporary driver is used repeatedly, review the note after a few deliveries and remove fields nobody fills in. A note with empty columns teaches staff to skip sections. Decide who reviews the completed notes and how often, and write that into the note header as a simple line. For accommodation operators in Sarawak, the practical value is continuity: a new staff member or a relief driver should be able to read one page and understand what happened without asking around. Common gaps to check before you share the note

Before the note leaves your property, run a short check. Is the provider confirmation attached and dated? Is the accepting person named as a role and as an individual? Is the temporary driver recorded only as the carrier? Does every incident line have an owner and a next action? Are there any guest names, room occupant details or personal contact details that should not be in a shared operational record? Remove them. Is the note stored where the people who need it can find it, and is editing restricted to the roles you have agreed? Finally, check that the note does not imply a guarantee, a replacement promise or a conclusion about who is responsible. Those questions belong in your written correspondence with the provider, guided by the documents you already hold. A clean handoff note is short, factual and repeatable. If a section has never been used across several deliveries, consider deleting it rather than leaving it to be misunderstood. Review the note at the same time you review your linen stock records so the two stay consistent.
