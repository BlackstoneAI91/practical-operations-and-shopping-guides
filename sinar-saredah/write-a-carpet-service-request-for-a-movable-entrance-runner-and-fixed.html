Writing a Carpet Service Request for a Movable Entrance Runner and a Fixed Floor Covering

Published by Sinar Saredah Sdn Bhd.

A practical guide for Sarawak households, accommodation operators and venue managers on separating measurements, ownership and permission to assess when requesting carpet cleaning for a movable entrance runner and a fixed floor covering, then recording outstanding questions before any work is agreed.

Start by separating the two items in your request

A movable entrance runner and a fixed floor covering behave differently in a service request, even when they sit a few metres apart. The runner can be lifted, counted and described as a single item. The fixed covering belongs to the room and cannot be treated as a loose object. Write them as two separate entries from the first line of your enquiry, because a provider reading a combined description has to guess which details apply to which surface.

Use a simple two-column layout. In the left column, describe the movable runner: where it normally sits, its approximate length and width, how many pieces there are, and who owns it. In the right column, describe the fixed covering: the room or zone name, the approximate floor area, and who has authority to approve work on it. Keep the two columns apart even if the same person will answer for both.

This separation matters for households, where the runner may be a personal purchase and the fixed carpet part of a rented unit, and for venue managers, where a runner may belong to an event supplier while the fixed covering belongs to the building. Treat that as the frame for your written request, not as a substitute for your own item list.

Record measurements as estimates with a stated method

Measurements in a first request are working figures, not a survey. Say so. Write each figure with the method you used, for example a tape measure along the runner, a room plan for the fixed area, or a count of tiles. If you are estimating, label it as an estimate so the provider knows what still needs confirming.

For the movable runner, record length, width and the number of pieces. Note whether edges are bound, fringed or loose, because that affects how the item is handled and described, not how it is cleaned. For the fixed covering, record the room name, the approximate area and any clear boundaries such as a doorway threshold or a change in flooring. Avoid describing the whole building as one area; a zone name that matches your own floor plan is easier to confirm later.

Keep a short note of who measured and when. If two people measured different rooms, list both names against their entries. This is a documentation habit, not a claim about accuracy. Where a figure is uncertain, write the question beside it rather than rounding silently. A request that shows its own gaps is easier to answer than one that looks complete but cannot be checked.

Establish ownership and permission before anything is agreed

Ownership decides who can approve a request. For a movable runner, ask who bought it and who is authorised to send it for cleaning. For a fixed floor covering, ask who controls the space: the household, the landlord, the building manager or the venue operator. Write the answer as a named role, not a guess.

Where the two answers differ, note the split clearly. A tenant may own the runner but need permission for the fixed covering. An event supplier may own the runner while the venue owns the floor. In both cases, the request should state which party has confirmed and which is still pending. Do not assume that paying for a service implies authority over the surface.

Describe condition without prescribing a method

Your job in the request is to describe what you see and what you need assessed, not to choose a cleaning method. Use plain observations: visible marks, traffic areas, colour differences between the runner and the fixed covering, loose edges, or an odour you noticed. Note where each observation sits, using your zone names.

Avoid instructions such as which product to use or how to lift the runner. Those decisions belong to the provider after assessment. Instead, write questions: whether the runner can be assessed as a separate item, whether the fixed covering needs a site visit, and what information the provider wants before answering. This keeps the request honest about what you know.

For households, a short list of observations is usually enough. For accommodation operators and venue managers, add who recorded the observation and when, so the note can be matched to a room or a date. If photographs exist, describe where they are stored rather than attaching them to every message. The goal is a request the provider can read once and understand, with the open questions visible at the end.

Write the outstanding questions as a numbered list

End the request with a numbered list of questions. This is the part a provider can answer directly, and it keeps the request from turning into a conversation with no record. Keep each question to one point.

Useful questions include: which item details still need confirming, whether a site assessment is required for the fixed covering, who should approve each item, what written confirmation will be issued, and how the runner will be identified if it leaves the premises. Add any question about timing that matters to you, phrased as a request for confirmation rather than an assumption.

Keep the request and the response together

A request is only useful if the answer can be found later. Store the original request, the provider's written reply and any quotation or confirmation in one place, labelled with the date and the room or item it concerns. For a movable runner, label by item. For a fixed covering, label by room or zone.

Where a venue or accommodation operator is involved, decide who holds the master copy and who receives a summary. Avoid scattering updates across messages with no owner. If a detail changes, such as a corrected measurement or a new permission, add a dated note rather than editing the original silently.

A short pre-send checklist

Before sending, run through a short checklist. Are the runner and the fixed covering written as separate entries? Does each entry have a measurement with its method and an estimate label where needed? Is ownership stated for each item, with the approving role named? Are observations separated from questions? Is there a numbered list of outstanding points? Is there a note of where the request and reply will be stored?

Score the checklist as a personal habit, not a standard. A request that answers most points is usually easier to act on than one that answers none, but the right threshold depends on your situation. For a single household runner, a few lines may be enough. For a venue with a fixed covering and several rooms, a fuller record is worth the effort.

If you are comparing options, keep the same structure for each provider so the replies can be read side by side.
