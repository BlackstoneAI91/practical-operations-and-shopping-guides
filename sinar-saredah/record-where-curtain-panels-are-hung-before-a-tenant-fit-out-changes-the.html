Record Where Curtain Panels Are Hung Before a Tenant Fit-Out Changes the Plan

Published by Sinar Saredah Sdn Bhd.

A practical guide for Sarawak households, accommodation operators and venue managers on documenting curtain panel placement, temporary storage authorisation and handoff to the building owner before a tenant fit-out changes the plan.

Why a Written Placement Baseline Matters Before Fit-Out

A tenant fit-out rarely begins with curtains in mind. Plans move walls, shift window openings and rename rooms, and the panels that were measured for the old layout can end up in a storage cupboard with no record of which window they belonged to. The practical answer is to write a placement baseline before any fit-out conversation starts: a simple list that pairs each panel with the room, the window it serves and the rail position it was hung on.

This is a documentation task, not a hardware task. You are not deciding how panels attach or which track to buy. You are recording what exists now so that a later handoff to the building owner, a cleaning provider or a returning tenant can be checked against something concrete. Keep the baseline short enough that someone unfamiliar with the building can read it in a few minutes.

Where cleaning is part of the plan, the baseline also supports a clearer conversation with a provider. Build a Panel Register With Four Columns

A useful register needs only four columns, and each one answers a question a fit-out team will eventually ask.

Panel reference: a short label you can repeat verbally and in writing, such as P-01 or a room-plus-number combination. Avoid labels that depend on a room name likely to change.

Current location: the room and window as they are named today, plus a plain description of the rail position, for example left of the doorway or facing the courtyard. Describe position, not fixing method.

Distinguishing feature: a neutral note such as colour family, lining presence or a visible repair mark. This helps match a returned panel without relying on memory.

Status: in place, removed for cleaning, held in temporary storage, or awaiting a decision.

Add a fifth field only if you need it: who authorised the current status. That single field prevents the most common confusion, where a panel is moved for cleaning and nobody remembers who agreed to it. Keep the register in one shared location and note the date of each update. A register that lives in two places will disagree with itself within a week.

Authorise Temporary Storage in Writing

Removing panels for a fit-out or for curtain cleaning creates a gap between the room and the item. During that gap, someone must be able to say where the panels are, who can move them and when they are expected back. Put that in a short storage authorisation note rather than leaving it in a message thread.

The note should name the panels by reference, state the storage location in general terms, name the person or role authorised to release them, and give the expected return window. It should also say what happens if the fit-out runs late. You do not need to predict the delay; you need to record who decides when the plan changes.

This is also the point to confirm the written terms that govern the items while they are out of the room. Reading those documents before authorising storage keeps the internal note and the provider's paperwork describing the same arrangement.

Separate Placement Records From Cleaning Records

Placement and cleaning are two different records, and merging them causes errors. The placement record answers where a panel belongs. The cleaning record answers what was sent, what came back and what condition it was in. A panel can be correctly cleaned and still be rehung in the wrong room if the placement record is vague.

Keep the two linked by the panel reference only. The cleaning side can then carry its own details, such as the collection date and the written confirmation received, without duplicating room descriptions that may change during the fit-out.

For operators managing several rooms, this separation also makes a combined handoff easier. Curtains and carpets are often collected together but reviewed against different criteria, so a shared reference number is more useful than a shared description. If your building also schedules commercial cleaning, keep that schedule in its own document too, with its own access notes and confirmation trail. The goal is one reference system and several focused records, not one overloaded spreadsheet.

A Short Handoff Note for the Building Owner

When the fit-out changes the plan, the building owner or incoming tenant needs a handoff note that stands on its own. Write it as a one-page summary with three parts.

What was recorded: the date of the baseline, the number of panels listed and the rooms covered.

What changed: the rooms or windows affected by the fit-out, and which panel references are now unassigned or awaiting a decision.

What is outstanding: panels in temporary storage, panels sent for curtain cleaning, and the name of the role responsible for the next confirmation.

Avoid conclusions the record cannot support. If a panel's future position is undecided, say so. If a returned panel has not been checked against its reference, mark it as pending rather than assumed correct. A handoff note that admits two open questions is more useful than one that closes them without evidence.

Date the note and keep earlier versions. Fit-out plans change more than once, and the version history is often the only way to explain why a panel ended up where it did.

Match the Record to the Provider's Written Confirmation

Before any panel leaves the building, compare your register with the written confirmation you receive. The two documents should agree on the panel references, the count and the stated condition at collection. Where they differ, record the difference as an open question rather than resolving it verbally.

This comparison is a reading exercise, not a judgement about cleaning quality. You are checking that the paperwork describes the same items you listed. If a confirmation uses a different naming convention, add a mapping line to your register so the two systems can be read side by side.

A Pre-Fit-Out Checklist You Can Score

Use this as an author's checklist, not a certified audit. Score each line as done, partial or open, and treat a score of anything less than all-done as a reason to pause the handoff conversation rather than a reason to proceed.

One: every panel has a reference that does not depend on a room name likely to change.

Two: each reference is paired with a current location and a neutral distinguishing feature.

Three: temporary storage has a written authorisation naming who can release the panels.

Four: cleaning records and placement records are linked by reference only.

Five: the handoff note states what changed and what remains outstanding.

Six: the provider's written confirmation has been compared with the register and differences are logged.

Seven: earlier versions of the register and handoff note are retained with dates.

Run the checklist twice: once before the fit-out plan is agreed and once before panels return to rooms. The second pass catches the changes that happened in between, which is exactly the moment a placement baseline earns its keep.
