Wafer Reclaim Paperwork for Reconciling a Split Collection Shipment

Published by San Techno.

A documentation guide for Singapore procurement and operations teams reconciling a wafer reclaim lot that leaves in two collections, covering lot division, two collection references, handover acknowledgement and an example discrepancy record.

Why a split collection needs one lot identity, not two

When a reclaim lot is too large or too awkward for one vehicle, the collection is often divided. The paperwork risk is not the second vehicle. It is the moment the split is treated as two unrelated lots. Once that happens, two sets of documents grow their own quantities, their own references and their own version of what was handed over, and reconciling them later becomes guesswork.

The practical rule is simple: one original lot identity, two collection references beneath it. Every document produced after the split should still carry the original lot or consignment reference, with a suffix or sub-reference identifying which collection it belongs to. That way the receiving side can total the two collections back to the original quantity without renegotiating what the lot was.

This matters most for wafer reclaim, where the material is counted, packed and tracked carefully. San Techno offers silicon wafer reclaim services as part of its semiconductor silicon materials work, and its public pages describe wafer inventory being handled through storage, packing and shipment preparation. That is a useful reminder that the physical handling and the paperwork should describe the same movement.

Dividing the original lot on paper

Before the first vehicle arrives, write down the division. A short lot division note is usually enough, and it should answer four questions: what was the original lot reference and quantity, how is it being split, which collection reference covers which portion, and who authorised the split.

Keep the division expressed in the same unit the original lot used. If the lot was recorded in wafer counts, do not switch to weight for one collection and count for the other unless the conversion is documented and agreed. Mixed units are one of the most common reasons two collection records fail to add up.

A worked example, entirely hypothetical: an original lot reference of LOT-A-100 is recorded as 400 wafers. Collection one is assigned reference LOT-A-100-C1 for 250 wafers and collection two is assigned LOT-A-100-C2 for 150 wafers. The division note records 250 plus 150 equals 400, names the person who approved the split, and is dated before the first pickup. Nothing in that note is a claim about how any particular supplier operates; it is simply a record that keeps the arithmetic visible.

If the material includes non-usable silicon being channelled back into the value chain, the same discipline applies. San Techno's public material describes helping recover non-usable silicon from integrated-circuit fabs, silicon wafer manufacturers, solar cell companies and chip packaging facilities, so a split collection may involve material from more than one internal source. Where that is the case, the division note should also show which source each portion came from.

Two collection references and who acknowledges handover

Each collection should have its own collection reference, its own date and time, its own vehicle or carrier detail where your process records one, and its own handover acknowledgement. The acknowledgement is the point where responsibility for the material passes, and it should be signed or recorded by a named person on each side, not by a role title alone.

A practical pattern is to keep the original lot reference on every acknowledgement alongside the collection sub-reference. That lets a reviewer see at a glance that two acknowledgements belong to one lot. It also means that if one acknowledgement is missing, the gap is obvious rather than hidden inside a combined total.

Decide in advance who is authorised to acknowledge handover for each collection. If the same person signs both, record that. If different people sign, record both names and the reason. Avoid leaving the acknowledgement field blank on the assumption that the delivery note covers it; a delivery note and a handover acknowledgement answer different questions.

For teams handling material that is being routed toward recovery rather than reuse, the same acknowledgement discipline applies. San Techno's public pages describe silicon recovery as channelling non-usable silicon from fabs, wafer makers, cell producers and packaging facilities back into the value chain, and the collection record is what ties a specific movement to that route.

An example discrepancy record

Discrepancies are normal in split collections, and the useful response is a short written record rather than a verbal note. A discrepancy record should state the original lot reference, the collection reference affected, what was expected, what was observed, the difference, who identified it, when, and what action was agreed.

A hypothetical example: collection LOT-A-100-C2 was expected to contain 150 wafers and 148 were counted at handover. The record notes the two-wafer difference, the date and time of the count, the name of the person who counted, and the agreed next step, such as a recount before the material is processed further. It does not speculate about cause. Cause can be investigated separately; the record's job is to fix the facts while they are fresh.

Keep discrepancy records with the collection documents, not in a separate folder that drifts out of sync. When the two collections are reconciled back to the original lot, the discrepancy record should be visible next to the acknowledgement it qualifies. That way a reviewer sees the difference and the agreed action together.

This is author guidance, not a standard or a certification. Adapt the fields to your own quality and procurement procedures.

When intake records clarify a disposal or recycling inquiry

Split collection paperwork often gets pulled into a different conversation later: someone asks whether a batch should be treated as a disposal matter or a recycling matter. Intake records help here because they show what actually arrived, in what quantity, under which reference, and with what condition notes.

A clean intake record lets the team answer the question from evidence rather than memory. If the material is a candidate for recovery, the intake record supports that route. San Techno's public material notes that recovered materials can be processed into reusable raw materials for solar wafers and cells, extending their useful life and reducing unnecessary resource loss. That is a statement about what the company describes doing with recovered material, not a promise about any specific batch.

Where the inquiry is really about disposal rather than recovery, the intake record still helps, because it establishes the quantity and condition that any downstream decision depends on. The point is not to force every inquiry into one route. It is to make sure the paperwork can support whichever route is chosen, and that the split collection has not left the record ambiguous.

A short reconciliation checklist

Use this as a starting checklist, adapted to your own procedures. It is author guidance, not an audited standard.

One: the original lot reference and quantity are recorded once, in one unit.

Two: a dated division note shows how the lot was split and who approved it.

Three: each collection has its own sub-reference that still carries the original lot reference.

Four: each collection has a handover acknowledgement with a named person on each side.

Five: the two collection quantities add back to the original lot quantity, or a discrepancy record explains the difference.

Six: any discrepancy record sits with the collection documents it qualifies.

Seven: intake records are filed so that a later disposal or recycling inquiry can be answered from them.

Eight: the whole pack is reviewed by someone who did not prepare it, before the lot is closed.

Keeping the pack usable after the lot closes

The value of split collection paperwork shows up months later, when someone needs to explain a quantity, trace a batch or answer an audit question. A pack that reconciles cleanly at close is far easier to use than one that was assembled from memory afterwards.

A simple habit helps: at close, attach a one-page reconciliation summary to the front of the pack. It should show the original lot reference, the two collection references, the quantities, the acknowledgements, any discrepancy records and the final reconciled total. Anyone opening the pack then sees the whole movement before reading the detail.

Keep the summary factual. It should not restate commercial terms, internal approvals beyond the split authorisation, or anything marked confidential. The aim is a clear record of a physical movement, not a second commercial file.

Finally, treat the pack as a living document until the lot is formally closed. If a recount changes a figure, update the summary and the discrepancy record together, and note who made the change and when. That small discipline is what makes a split collection reconcilable rather than merely documented.
