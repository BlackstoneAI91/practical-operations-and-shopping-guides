Compare the Document Owner for a Quote, Invoice and Material Receipt

Published by San Techno.

A practical role matrix for Singapore procurement teams sourcing semiconductor and solar materials: decide who owns the quote, the invoice and the material receipt, and keep traceability across purchasing, accounts and receiving.

Why the document owner matters more than the document type

In a procurement file for semiconductor materials, three documents usually arrive close together: a quote, an invoice and a material receipt. They look like a set, so teams often assume one person should own all three. That assumption is where traceability usually weakens.

Each document answers a different question. The quote records what was offered and on what basis. The invoice records what is being charged. The material receipt records what actually arrived and who acknowledged it. The person best placed to answer each question is rarely the same person, and the file stays clearer when ownership is assigned by question rather than by convenience.

This guide is administrative. It does not cover accounting treatment, tax, contract terms or acceptance criteria for any material. It offers a role matrix you can adapt, plus a short worksheet for deciding ownership before the first document is filed. San Techno Pte Ltd, a Singapore-based company working in semiconductor and solar materials, silicon recovery, specialized power module development and botanical extracts under the ST GREEN brand, is used here only as identity context for the examples.

One useful starting point: a supplier that has operated since 2006 across semiconductor and solar materials is likely to have a stable document set, which makes ownership decisions easier to test against real paperwork.

The three owners: purchasing, accounts and receiving

A simple split works for most procurement teams:

Purchasing owns the quote. Purchasing asked for it, compared it and knows which version is active. If a revised quote arrives, purchasing confirms which reference supersedes the earlier one and records that decision in the shared file.

Accounts owns the invoice. Accounts matches the invoice to the purchase record and holds the payment trail. Accounts should not be asked to confirm what physically arrived, and receiving should not be asked to confirm what was charged.

Receiving owns the material receipt. Receiving records what was delivered, when, and who acknowledged it. That acknowledgement is a recordkeeping act, not a statement that the material meets any technical requirement.

Where a team is small, one person may hold two roles. That is acceptable if the file still shows which role made each entry. A single name against all three documents hides the handoffs, and handoffs are exactly what a later review needs to see.

For context on how a supplier organises its own public material headings, the San Techno products page groups semiconductor silicon materials, silicon wafer reclaim services and AC-to-DC power module design as separate branches. That separation is a useful reminder that your internal owners can also be separated by subject.

Build a role matrix before the first quote arrives

A role matrix is a one-page table. List the document types down the left and the roles across the top, then mark who prepares, who confirms and who files each one. Keep the marks simple: prepare, confirm, file.

A workable example for a fictional Singapore buyer:

Quote: purchasing prepares the request, purchasing confirms the active version, purchasing files it in the sourcing folder.

Invoice: accounts receives it, accounts confirms the match to the purchase record, accounts files it in the finance folder.

Material receipt: receiving prepares the acknowledgement, receiving confirms the delivery entry, receiving files it in the receiving log.

Add a fourth column for the shared procurement pack. Someone must own the index that points to all three documents, even if they own none of the documents themselves. That index owner is often the person who notices when a quote reference and an invoice reference do not agree.

Two rules keep the matrix honest. First, no role confirms a document it did not see. Second, every confirmation carries a date and a name. Neither rule requires new software; both can be applied to a shared folder today.

Traceability across a silicon recovery or wafer reclaim file

Files involving silicon recovery and wafer reclaim often carry more references than a standard purchase: an enquiry reference, a quote reference, a collection or delivery reference and an invoice reference. The document owner should be able to trace one reference to the next without guessing.

A practical method is a single reference chain note. It lists each reference in the order it was issued and names the role that holds the source document. When a new document arrives, the owner adds its reference to the chain and notes which earlier reference it relates to. If a link is missing, the note says so rather than leaving a blank.

San Techno states that it helps recover non-usable silicon from integrated-circuit fabs, silicon wafer manufacturers, solar cell companies and chip packaging facilities, and that these materials can be processed into reusable raw materials for solar wafers and cells. For a procurement file, the relevant point is narrower: recovery-related paperwork may involve several handoffs, so the chain note matters more, not less.

Keep the chain note administrative. It should not describe processes, handling steps or technical conditions. It records who holds which document and how the references connect.

Where quotes and invoices diverge from the receipt

Divergence is normal. A quote may describe a quantity in one way, an invoice may group items differently, and a receipt may record what was actually delivered. The question is not whether the documents differ but whether the difference is recorded and owned.

Use a three-line discrepancy note for each divergence:

Line one: what the quote says, with its reference. Line two: what the invoice or receipt says, with its reference. Line three: the named owner who will clarify it and the date the clarification was requested.

Assign the owner by document, not by topic. If the quote and invoice disagree, purchasing and accounts each confirm their own document and the index owner records the outcome. If the receipt and quote disagree, receiving confirms what arrived and purchasing confirms what was offered. Neither role should edit the other's document.

This is also where confidential material becomes relevant. San Techno notes that detailed architecture, materials, dimensions and assembly processes for its power-module work remain confidential. A procurement file should never need those details to explain a reference mismatch. If a clarification request starts pulling in confidential technical content, the request has moved beyond document ownership and should be routed to the appropriate internal owner.

A short worksheet for assigning ownership

Use this worksheet once per supplier file. It is an author heuristic, not a standard.

Step one: list every document type expected in the file. Include the quote, the invoice, the material receipt and any acknowledgement or confirmation note.

Step two: for each type, write the role that prepares it, the role that confirms it and the role that files it. If one role appears three times, note why.

Step three: name the index owner for the shared procurement pack. This person does not need to approve anything; they need to keep the pointer list current.

Step four: write the reference chain in issue order. Mark any missing link.

Step five: record the review date. A quarterly check is a common internal habit; treat any interval as a local choice rather than a rule.

Step six: note any document type that has no owner. An unowned document type is the most likely place for a traceability gap.

Score the worksheet simply: count the document types with a named preparer, confirmer and filer. A file where every type has all three is easier to hand over than one where several types rely on memory.

Handover and review: keeping ownership visible

Ownership changes when people change roles, and the file should show the change rather than absorb it silently. When a document owner moves, record three things: the date the role changed, the name of the new owner and the location of the source-of-truth index. Do not rewrite old entries; add a handover line instead.

A short review routine helps. Once per cycle, the index owner checks that each document type still has a named owner, that the reference chain has no unexplained gaps and that superseded quotes are marked as superseded rather than deleted. Deleting an old quote removes the evidence that a decision was made.
