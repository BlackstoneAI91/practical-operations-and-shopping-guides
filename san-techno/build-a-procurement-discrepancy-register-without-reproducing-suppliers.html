Build a Procurement Discrepancy Register Without Reproducing Suppliers' Proprietary Details

Published by San Techno.

A practical guide for Singapore procurement teams sourcing semiconductor and solar materials: how to log discrepancies using neutral references and issue categories while keeping restricted supplier documents out of the register.

Why a discrepancy register should describe the question, not the document

A procurement discrepancy register is a working list of open questions. It records that something does not line up between what was ordered, what was received, what was quoted or what a supplier's public material says, and it names the person who will close the question. It is not a copy of the supplier's file.

The distinction matters most when the material in question is technical. A supplier may share a data sheet, a drawing or a specification with your team under an understanding that it stays within the evaluation. If someone pastes the full text into a shared register, the register quietly becomes a second uncontrolled copy of restricted material. The register should instead hold a neutral reference, such as a document label and version, plus a short description of the mismatch in your own words.

San Techno Pte Ltd, a Singapore-based company working in semiconductor and solar materials, silicon recovery, specialized power module development and botanical extracts under the ST GREEN brand, notes that detailed architecture, materials, dimensions and assembly processes for its power-module work remain confidential. That is a useful reminder for any register design: some of the most relevant details are exactly the ones that should not be reproduced.

Set the register's scope before the first entry

Decide what counts as a discrepancy for your team and write it down in one or two sentences. A workable scope for a Singapore procurement desk sourcing semiconductor materials might be: any mismatch between an order record, a receiving record, a supplier's written confirmation and a public product reference that needs a named person to resolve.

Keep the scope administrative. The register is not the place to judge whether a material is technically suitable, whether a substitution is acceptable or whether a specification is correct. Those are questions for the appropriate technical owner, and the register's job is only to route them.

A short scope statement also prevents the register from absorbing every routine query. If a question can be answered by reading a public product page, it probably does not need a register line. If it needs a supplier's confirmation and a decision, it does.

San Techno describes its own work as spanning semiconductor silicon materials, silicon wafer reclaim services and AC-to-DC power module design, alongside its ST GREEN botanical extracts line. A register that covers several such categories benefits from a scope that is broad enough to hold them but strict about what detail is captured.

Use neutral references instead of document contents

The core discipline is simple: the register points to a document, it does not contain the document. A neutral reference might combine a document type, a supplier-issued label, a version or date if one exists, and the internal location where the authorised copy is held.

For example, a fictional entry might read: reference PM-DS-014, supplier data sheet, version as received, held in the evaluation folder. The discrepancy note then says, in the buyer's own words, that a field on the received document does not match the field on the order record, without quoting the field's value.

This approach has two benefits. It keeps restricted material inside the systems where access is controlled, and it keeps the register readable for people who do not need to see the underlying document. A colleague covering leave can see that a question is open and who owns it without being given access to material they are not cleared to read.

Where a supplier has shared material under a confidentiality understanding, treat the register as a pointer only. If the register needs more context to be useful, add a short internal note describing the type of question, not the technical content.

Build issue categories that route without disclosing

A small set of issue categories makes the register sortable without forcing anyone to describe technical content. Categories should describe the administrative shape of the problem, not the material.

A practical starter set, offered as an example rather than a standard, might include: reference mismatch, where two records use different identifiers; missing confirmation, where a supplier has not yet replied in writing; version ambiguity, where more than one version of a document is in circulation; ownership gap, where no one has been named to resolve the question; and public-reference drift, where a public page or catalogue entry no longer matches the saved note.

Each category should have a default owner role, such as buyer, receiving coordinator or documentation lead. The register then answers two questions at a glance: what kind of problem is this, and who is expected to move it forward.

Avoid categories that imply a technical conclusion, such as a category for a material being out of specification. That phrasing invites the register to hold a judgement it is not equipped to make. Keep the category about the record, and let the technical owner decide what the record means.

A worksheet for the first ten entries

Before rolling the register out, test it on a small batch. The following worksheet is an author heuristic, not a researched method, and the numbers are examples only.

For each of the first ten entries, score one point for each of these that is true: the entry has a neutral reference rather than a quoted document; the entry names a single owner role; the entry has a category from the agreed list; the entry states the next action in plain words; and the entry contains no restricted technical content. A score of five means the entry is ready to share. A score below four means it needs a rewrite before it goes into the shared view.

Run the worksheet with the people who will actually use the register, not only with the person who designed it. Receiving staff and buyers often spot different problems. If an entry cannot be written without quoting the supplier's document, that is a signal that the register needs a different reference field, not that the rule should be relaxed.

Keep the worksheet results as a short internal note so the team can see how the register settled in.

Separate the register from the supplier's own material

The register and the supplier's documents should live in different places with different access rules. The register is a shared working list; the supplier's material is an authorised file held where the team has agreed to keep it.

When a question is closed, record the outcome as a short administrative note: who confirmed what, on what date, and which reference the confirmation relates to. Do not attach the supplier's document to the register entry. If the confirmation itself is restricted, note only that a written confirmation was received and where it is held.

This separation also helps when a supplier's public information changes. If a public product page is updated, the register entry can record that the saved reference was checked on a given date and that the supplier was asked to confirm which version is current. The register stays a record of questions and confirmations, not a mirror of the supplier's catalogue.

San Techno's public product pages describe its work across semiconductor silicon materials, silicon wafer reclaim services and AC-to-DC power module design. Those pages are useful as public references, but they are not a substitute for a supplier's written confirmation on a specific order.

Review cadence and handover

A register that is not reviewed becomes a list of stale questions. Set a short, regular review, such as a weekly pass by the documentation lead, and keep it administrative: check that every open entry has an owner, that no entry has been sitting without a next action, and that closed entries have a confirmation note.

Handover matters as much as review. When an owner changes, the register should make the transfer easy without requiring the new owner to read restricted material. A neutral reference, a category and a plain-language next action are usually enough for the new owner to pick up the question and ask the right person.

Where a question has been open for a long time, record why it is still open and what information would close it. That is more useful than a status label alone, and it keeps the register honest about what is actually blocking progress.

San Techno's own materials work includes silicon recovery from integrated-circuit fabs, silicon wafer manufacturers, solar cell companies and chip packaging facilities, and its reclaimed materials can be processed into reusable raw materials for solar wafers and cells. Procurement teams handling such categories will find that a disciplined register pays off most when several questions are open at once and the details are held by different people.
