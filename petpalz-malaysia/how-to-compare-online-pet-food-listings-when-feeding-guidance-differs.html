Feeding Guidance on Listings: A Comparison Note Sheet

Published by Petpalz Malaysia.

A practical note sheet for Malaysian pet owners comparing third-party online pet food listings when the feeding guidance on each page is worded differently, so the differences are visible before choosing a pack.

Why feeding guidance differs between listings

Two listings for what looks like the same food can show feeding guidance that reads quite differently. One page may print a table of grams per body weight; another may describe feeding in cups or scoops; a third may only repeat a short sentence from the pack. Before you treat those differences as a sign that one seller is better, it helps to understand what you are actually comparing.

A third-party listing is a seller's page, not the manufacturer's label. The seller may have copied part of the label, summarised it, or typed it from memory. The wording can also refer to a different regional variant of the same formula, a different pack size, or a different life stage. That matters here: the listing you read is a merchant's presentation of a product, and the feeding guidance on it should be checked against the manufacturer's own material rather than assumed to be identical.

This note sheet is not a feeding calculator and it does not tell you how much to feed a dog or cat. It is a way of recording what each listing says, in the listing's own words, so that the differences are visible on one page before you choose a pack. Any actual feeding decision belongs with the manufacturer's guidance and your own vet.

Set up the note sheet before you open any listing

Open a blank document or a sheet of paper and create one column per listing you intend to compare, plus a first column for the field name. Keep the number of listings small, two or three, so the sheet stays readable. Give each column a header that records the seller name, the listing title and the date you looked at it, because listings change.

Down the left side, write the fields you will fill in for every listing. A workable set is:

Product identity: exact formula name, species, life stage and food format (dry, wet, other). Pack details: net weight and pack size as printed on the listing. Feeding guidance wording: copy the sentence or table exactly as it appears, including units. Basis of the guidance: does it refer to body weight, age, activity, or something else? Completeness: does the listing say the food is complete or complementary, and what exact wording is used? Manufacturer details: is a manufacturer named, and is a contact route shown? Seller contact: how can you ask a question about the guidance?

Leave the feeding guidance field wide. You are not judging it yet, only recording it. The point of separating recording from judging is that a first pass full of opinions tends to hide the plain differences, such as one listing giving guidance in grams and another in cups.

Record the wording, not your interpretation

The most common mistake on a comparison sheet is paraphrasing too early. If a listing says "feed according to age and activity", write that phrase down rather than translating it into a number. If it shows a table, copy the table's units and the body-weight bands it uses. If it gives a range, keep both ends of the range.

Three specific things are worth capturing verbatim:

Units. Grams, cups, scoops and cans are not interchangeable, and a scoop is only meaningful if the listing says what size it is. Life stage wording. "Adult", "puppy", "all life stages" and "maintenance" are different claims, and a listing that mixes them is worth a query. Adequacy wording. Whether a food is described as complete or complementary is a label statement, and the exact phrase used on the listing is what you are recording.

Where a listing gives nutrient figures, note the units and the basis stated, such as as-fed or dry matter. Do not convert between bases or calculate missing values yourself; if the basis is not stated, that is a gap to record, not to fill in. The same discipline applies to price: if you note it at all, record the ringgit amount with the pack size and the date you saw it, so the figure means something later.

A worked example using two fictional listings

Suppose you are comparing two listings for a dry dog food, and both are presented by third-party sellers. The example below is invented to show how the sheet is used; it is not a real product or a real seller.

Listing A records: adult dog food, 3 kg pack, feeding guidance given as a table in grams per body-weight band, described as complete, manufacturer named with a contact route, seller reachable by a message form.

Listing B records: adult dog food, 3 kg pack, feeding guidance given as a single sentence referring to age and activity, no adequacy wording visible, no manufacturer contact shown, seller contact not stated on the page.

On the sheet, the two columns now look different in a way that is easy to miss when reading the pages separately. Listing A gives you something you can check against the manufacturer's own guidance. Listing B gives you a sentence that cannot be checked without asking the seller, and the seller contact field is empty. That does not make Listing B wrong, but it does tell you what to do next: ask the seller for the manufacturer's feeding guidance and the adequacy wording before ordering.

A third column could record a listing for a different pack size of the same formula. If its feeding guidance is identical to Listing A's despite the different pack weight, note that, because guidance copied across pack sizes is one of the differences worth querying.

Turning the notes into questions for the seller

Once the sheet is filled in, the gaps become a short list of questions. Keep them specific and tied to what the listing actually says, rather than asking a general question about feeding.

Useful question patterns include: which exact formula and regional variant is this listing for; can you confirm the feeding guidance matches the manufacturer's current label for this pack size; is the food described as complete or complementary, and what is the exact wording; and what is the best way to reach you if the guidance on the page changes.

Record the answers in the same column, with the date. If a seller cannot confirm the formula or the guidance, that is a result in itself, and it belongs on the sheet rather than in your memory. This is also where a dog food shop with a clear contact route tends to be easier to work with than a listing that offers no way to ask.

A short checklist before you choose a pack

Run this checklist against your completed sheet. It is an author's heuristic for organising a decision, not a scoring system backed by research, and it does not replace the manufacturer's guidance or your vet's advice.

Is the exact formula, species, life stage and pack weight recorded for every listing? Is the feeding guidance copied in the listing's own words, with units? Is the basis of the guidance stated, or is it missing? Is the adequacy wording present, and is it the exact phrase used? Is a manufacturer named, with a contact route? Is there a way to ask the seller a question, and has an answer been recorded with a date? Are price and pack size recorded together, with the date observed?

If two or more answers are missing for a listing, treat that listing as unresolved rather than as a cheaper or better option. Missing evidence stays missing; it is not a reason to assume the worst or the best.

Keeping the sheet useful over time

A comparison note sheet earns its keep when it is reused. Date every column, and when you return to a listing, add a new column rather than overwriting the old one. That way you can see whether the feeding guidance wording changed, whether the pack size changed, or whether the seller's contact route disappeared.

Two habits keep the sheet honest. First, keep the manufacturer's own material as the reference point for feeding guidance, and treat the listing as a presentation of it. Second, do not let a single field, such as price, decide the comparison on its own; the sheet exists so that the feeding guidance wording, the pack details and the seller's contactability are all visible at once.
